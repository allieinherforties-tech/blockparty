NYC Block Party Permit Guide

How to get an NYC block party permit: eligibility, the E-Apply application, the 60-day deadline, the $25 fee, community board review, and what happens next.

Updated October 27, 2026

Short version: a block party on a closed Brooklyn street runs on a Street Activity Permit, filed through the city's E-Apply system at least 60 days before your date, with a $25 non-refundable fee. The permit itself is the organizer's job — that's been the deal since day one — and this page walks the whole process in order.

What counts as a block party (to the city)

New York City defines a block party as a community-sponsored, public event — one block, one day, no sale of goods or services, no fundraising, generally limited to nine consecutive hours (CECM's block party rules). That "no sales" line is the hinge: the moment money changes hands on the street, you're describing a street festival, which is a different permit with different requirements — including insurance and Health Department clearances.

Before you apply: two checks that prevent rejections

First, your community board. The citywide rule is 60 days, but boards run their own calendars on top of it — Brooklyn Community Board 2's guidance, for example, asks block parties to submit 90 days out, and Brooklyn CB9 set July 14 as the last day to apply for a summer 2026 party (CB9's block party page). Check your own board's page for its deadline and any extra paperwork.

Second, your police precinct. Some precincts keep lists of blocks that can't close and dates that are already spoken for — Brooklyn CB4, for instance, tells organizers to check the 83rd Precinct's street-closing denial and blocked-off date lists before applying (CB4's process). One look at that list can save you $25 and a summer of waiting.

The application, step by step

  1. Create an E-Apply account. Block party permits are filed online through the city's E-Apply system at nyc.gov — and if you've held a permit from SAPO or another city agency, you may already have the account.
  2. Choose "Block Party" as the event type, then enter your date and the sponsor contact. If a community organization is sponsoring, the contact should be a person in that organization, not "whoever answers the group chat fastest."
  3. Enter the location: the specific block you're closing.
  4. Fill out the questionnaire. Describe the event and list everything that will occupy the street, sidewalk, or curb lane — tables, games, the drink station. Honesty here is what keeps the agencies from surprises later.
  5. Upload what your board asks for. Some boards want resident signatures, a site plan, or an event schedule.
  6. Pay the $25 processing fee by credit or debit card. It's non-refundable — denied or withdrawn, it stays with the city (CECM's fee schedule).

From there the application routes to SAPO — the Street Activity Permit Office, now part of the Mayor's Office of Citywide Event Coordination and Management — along with the NYPD and your community board.

After you file: the review

Your community board reviews the application and weighs in before final approval, and SAPO coordinates with the NYPD and other city agencies. Answer document requests fast — a slow email thread is how August dates get lost. Approval isn't real until you're holding it.

The extras that trip people up

Amplified sound needs a separate NYPD sound permit, and sound permits generally expire at 7:00 p.m. — plan the speeches accordingly. Rides and some inflatables trigger insurance and clearance requirements. Food for the general public runs on its own Health Department track (covered here), and a tent or temporary structure over 10 feet tall can pull in the Department of Buildings. None of these are reasons to skip the party; they're just not on the main form.

Approved. Now what?

Download the permit from E-Apply, print it, and keep it on the block on party day — it's the document that answers "who said this was okay?" before anyone has to ask it twice.

Frequently asked

How far in advance do I need to apply for an NYC block party permit?

At least 60 days before your event, through the city's E-Apply system. Treat 60 days as the floor — some community boards ask for earlier filing on top of that.

How much does a block party permit cost in NYC?

A $25 non-refundable processing fee, paid online when you apply. The fee is not returned if your application is denied or withdrawn.

Can I sell food at an NYC block party?

Not under a standard block party permit — block parties are community events with no sales of goods or services. Events with vendors or food sales are a different permit type with Health Department requirements.

Do I need a separate permit for amplified sound at a block party?

Yes — amplified sound needs its own NYPD sound permit, separate from the street activity permit, and sound permits generally expire at 7:00 p.m.
