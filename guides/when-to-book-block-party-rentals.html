When to Book Block Party Rentals

The deadline math for a block party: the city's 60-day permit clock, our 2-week minimum lead, and why you book equipment the same week you file the permit.

Updated October 27, 2026

Two clocks run your block party: the city's and ours. One takes 60 days and doesn't bend; the other takes 2 weeks and a phone call. Here's how to make them line up.

The city's clock: 60 days, minimum

Block party permit applications go in at least 60 days before your date through the city's E-Apply system. That's the citywide floor — some community boards ask for more (Brooklyn Community Board 2's guidance says 90 days), and summer calendars can have hard cutoffs. The full sequence lives in our permit guide.

Our clock: 2 weeks, and it's a minimum, not a target

BlockParty asks for at least 2 weeks' lead before your event. That's what it takes to confirm your delivery window, your setup spot, and your pickup with you — the confirmation call is part of every booking, not an upsell. But 2 weeks is the minimum we ask for, not a promise that your first-choice setup is still sitting free. Book the week your permit filing goes in and the question never comes up.

The move: same-week booking

The permit application and the rental email can go out the same afternoon. One of them starts 60 days of city time; the other starts two weeks of, honestly, pretty light logistics. Organizer note: whoever fills out the E-Apply form should send us the email before closing the laptop.

What booking late actually costs

File at 60 days and book at 13, and the street still closes — with nothing on it. A closed block with no games is just a very public parking complaint. The fix costs nothing: book when you file.

Your date's real the moment the application's in. Pick the package, send the email, and get back to the group chat — you've done the hard parts.

Frequently asked

How far in advance should I book block party rentals?

BlockParty asks for at least 2 weeks' lead to confirm delivery windows and equipment availability. Book the same week you file your permit application — the permit is the long pole at 60 days, and early booking locks your equipment.

Is two weeks really enough lead time?

It's the minimum, not the target. Two weeks covers the logistics on our side; the 60-day permit clock and your community board's own deadlines are what actually pace the planning. Book as early as your date is real.
