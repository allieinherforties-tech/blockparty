Rain Plan for a Brooklyn Block Party

NYC doesn't do rain dates for block parties — build a wet-weather plan instead: the go/no-go call, what moves under cover, and how the rental fits in.

Updated October 27, 2026

First, the fact that shapes everything: New York City doesn't accept rain-date applications for block parties. Your permit is for one specific date — there's no "and if it rains, the following Saturday" box on the form. Check the forecast like it owes you money, and build the plan that works in both weathers.

The go/no-go call

Pick a decision time — morning of, early — and a decider: one person, not a committee of the whole group chat. Summer rain in Brooklyn is usually a passing shower, not an event-killer; the plan that works is "start late" or "shorten the day" more often than "cancel."

What moves, what stays

Under cover goes anything with a plug or a pour: the drink station first. Give the slushie machine the most covered spot on the block — shade is good practice on a sunny day anyway (more on drink-station setup here). The lawn games shake off a drizzle; pause play when the bags soak through or the Jenga stack goes slippery — nobody's proud moment happens with wet gear.

Canopies and the 10-foot line

A pop-up canopy keeps the food table usable and the adults upright. The rule to know: a temporary structure over 10 feet tall can pull in a Department of Buildings permit — so keep the setup modest and weighted, and your rain plan simple.

Frequently asked

Does NYC give rain dates for block parties?

No — the city doesn't accept rain-date or make-up-date applications for block parties. Your permit covers one specific date, so build a wet-weather plan instead of banking on a do-over.

What happens if it rains on my block party?

Light rain: cover the drink station and electronics, pause the lawn games, keep going. Steady rain: make the morning-of go/no-go call with your neighbors.
