What to Rent for Your Crowd Size

Matching block party rentals to your headcount: packages sized for 10–75 people, à la carte games from $65/day, and when a slushie machine or ball pit fits.

Updated October 27, 2026

Rent for the crowd you have, not the one you're picturing at peak. Here's the sizing we use when blocks ask us — and it's the same sizing our packages were built around.

Why packages beat picking items one by one

Arithmetic, mostly: the Classic trio books à la carte for $65 + $65 + $75 = $205, and the package is $185 with the same delivery, pickup, and confirmation call. Packages are also sized by the people who deliver the stuff — they're what we'll recommend on the call if they fit.

The à la carte calls that change a party

Hot day or long party: the slushie machine ($275/day single-flavor, $375/day double-flavor — one kids' flavor and one adult mix side by side). Blocks with a lot of under-10s: the ball pit ($325/day, supervision required). A block with teenagers: the portable hoop ($85/day) buys you four quiet hours. Every item works on closed pavement; each item page notes what it needs.

Two placement rules that matter more than quantity

First: two or three well-placed games beat five scattered ones — players rotate, and nobody wonders where the crowd is. Second: keep the games where people can see them from the food table. A game nobody can see from the food table is a game that plays twice all day.

Measure the block, count the kids, pick the tier — or email us and we'll size it with you on the confirmation call.

Frequently asked

What should I rent for 30 people at a block party?

The Full Block Party package ($420) is sized for roughly 25–50 people: cornhole, giant Jenga, giant beer pong, and a single-flavor slushie machine, delivered and picked up. Skew young? Add the ball pit. A teenager-heavy block? Add the portable basketball hoop.

How many games does a block party need?

Fewer than you think: two or three well-placed games rotate players all day. A regulation two-board cornhole set plays 2–4 at a time, and a game nobody can see from the food table plays twice all day.
